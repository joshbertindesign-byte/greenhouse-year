# Greenhouse Year

A greenhouse management game that runs in the browser. The year runs in eight periods. Between periods you plan orders on a bench-by-period board. During each period you and your crew pot, water, spray, fix and load trucks. The operation grows from one greenhouse to four side by side, then up to three floors on each: twelve houses in all.

The earlier quarterly version lives in `classic/`.

## Play

Open `index.html` in a browser, or play it on GitHub Pages: https://joshbertindesign-byte.github.io/greenhouse-year/

## How it plays

- **Planning:** offers come up one at a time, and you can see the next two. Place each one on the board or bin it.
- **Shapes:** each square on the board is one bench for one period. Most orders can be rotated between Standard, Tight and Spaced shapes.
- **Timing:** one-off orders can ship early but never late. Shipping early pays less, and shipping in a crop's peak season pays more.
- **Swaps:** placing an order over unplanted ones swaps them out. Breaking a promise from an earlier round costs reputation with that customer.
- **Customers:** each of the eight customers has a standing of Lost, Wary, Steady, Loyal or Partner. Better standing means better prices, more offers and contracts. You're asked before you knowingly drop a customer to a lower tier.
- **Work:** tap the icons over the greenhouse to queue jobs. Crew pick up work on their home floor and get faster with experience.
- **Potting before shipping:** only potted plants ship. A truck that docks for an order you haven't potted yet waits: the potting job's timer shortens to the truck's, tapping the truck queues the potting first, and loaders won't touch it until it's potted. If the truck gives up, the order fails.
- **Trucks:** trucks queue at the road. You start with one loading bay and buy up to three more in Equipment (they open at nursery levels 3, 6 and 10). Bay 1 is yours to work; every bay past the first needs its own loader, and a built bay with no loader sits closed behind a barrier. Each bay has a numbered sign at the road (green when open, red when closed), and the next bay you can buy is staked out on the grass. Under the planning board, a pickups row counts the trucks each period and turns amber or red when your staffed bays can't load them all in time, with a note on the fix (hire a loader, build a bay, or spread pickups out). A truck that comes up short pauses the game so you can buy plants in or ship short.
- **Upgrades:** eleven upgrades, from pots and soil to watering booms, grow lights, a forklift and ladybugs. Each one shows up in the greenhouses.
- **Your nursery:** name it when you start (or rename it from the Ledger tab). The name goes on a roadside sign, the tablet and every magazine ranking.
- **Goals and levels:** year one is a learning year with a guided tutorial: staying healthy (the year review) is the only goal, and passing it takes you to level 2. From year two each year also has a goal (full trucks, operating profit, sales, reputation or a magazine rating). Hitting it moves you up a level, through 20 levels from Backyard grower to Hall of fame, and unlocks more upgrades, varieties, houses and crew. Miss it and you try again next year.
- **Best Homes and Gardens:** at the end of each year a fictional magazine ranks the best nurseries out of five stars. The Ledger tab keeps every year's ranking and stats.
- **Showcase house:** your first four-star year unlocks a gold-framed showcase house. Show entries for orchids, roses, peonies and calla lilies come up with your offers. Only the grower can work in it, and the plants need misting, staking and deadheading on time; each late or missed job lowers the entry's grade. At year end every entry is judged together for a Best Homes and Gardens ribbon, from Honorable mention to Best in Show, with prize money.
- **Prize grower:** after three years of showcase judging and a Silver ribbon or better, you can hire a prize grower ($5,000 fee, $600 a period) to run the showcase house so you can move on. They start out distracted; one expensive horticulture class a year ($4,000 up to $30,000) raises their attentiveness, up to Best in Show quality.
- **Grower levels and skills:** every job your grower does personally earns experience (tree and showcase jobs count extra), up to level 30. Each level gives a skill point for the skill tree: Hustle (speed, Multitasker), Green thumb (plant care), People (customers and crew) and Luck (stars, coffee, plugs, breakdowns). Lower tiers open after 3 and 6 points in a branch; skills can be reset once a year at the homestead for 3 stars.
- **Vegetable starts:** from level 7, buy a vegetable nursery certificate. At the start of each year you can sign a spring vegetable contract, promising a customer benches of lettuce, cabbage, tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers and basil for early and late spring. Those orders come first in those periods and ship the period they're planted; each line you can't plant cuts the contract bonus by a third. A germination room makes vegetable plugs cheap, then free; organic certification raises vegetable prices but charges for every spray on vegetable benches.
- **Succulent house:** from level 12, a shop beside the greenhouses. Plant pups of five kinds; they need little care, keep growing from year to year (small, medium, specimen) and sell to customers who walk in. Customers ask for a size and sometimes a favorite kind: a perfect match tips, a size match pays full price, anything else sells for less. Six regulars have known tastes and visiting seasons.
- **Almanac:** read before each year starts (and any time from the Ledger, Shop tab or homestead): last year in review, the weather outlook, when your flowers sell best, the show calendar, trees coming ready, and the succulent consumer report with what's in, what's out and next year's early word.
- **Office manager and customer care:** from level 8, with the bigger homestead office, hire an office manager from the Crew tab. They hold up to two offers you can't fit for the next planning step, and can make amends with wary or lost customers. Anyone can send a thank-you gift or make a site visit (which takes the grower away for part of the next period) once a year per customer from the Customers tab.
- **Magazine ladder:** the year-end rating comes from Gardener Quarterly, then Greenhouse Monthly, Pro Growers Magazine and Prize Growers. Five stars moves you up a step (+5 stars once, and +1 star a year per step); two years in a row at two stars or less moves you down. Each step has stricter star thresholds, a higher profit bar and stronger rivals. Best Homes and Gardens still runs the showcase flower shows.
- **Festivals and decorations:** festivals open at nursery level 3 with two a year, three from level 6 and four from level 10, never two in the same period. The almanac lists them: Valentine's Day, Easter, Mother's Day, Fourth of July, Halloween and Christmas come up most often, with Lunar New Year, Arbor Day, the county fair, Día de los Muertos and a garden club tour now and then. Buy in for festival orders (or pumpkin patches and a Christmas tree lot in the tree field) and earn that festival's decoration. Decorations mount on each house's frame: decorating opens at level 3 with two spots per house (the doorway and a back-wall garland), and a back-wall piece opens at level 6, a side-wall garland at 9 and the side ledge at 12. Festival decorations, gold pieces and cat hats stay hidden until you earn them. A star-priced store sells more, mostly just for fun.
- **Daily visits:** open the game each day for a reward (stars or cash), up to day 7, then round again; miss a day and it starts over. Day 7 brings the next weekly prize: five one-of-a-kind decorations and four cat hats, and last of all the diamond trowel, which adds 3 flats to every bench you plant.
- **Mail:** letters turn up at random times, usually with a few stars inside.
- **Gold decorations and cat hats:** every second win of a festival brings its decoration in gold, with twice the boost, and unlocks that festival's hat for the cats (Santa hat, witch hat, bunny ears, cowboy hat and more). Cats wear them while the festival is on, or pick an everyday hat in the cat wardrobe. Hats are just for looks.
- **Tree field:** from level 5 a field opens beside the greenhouses. Plant a sapling for 1 star in spring or fall (Japanese maple, blue spruce, magnolia, red maple, live oak). Trees take 2 to 5 years and need a few jobs a year that only the grower can do; missed jobs lower the grade. Sell grown trees to a landscaper for cash or to the county arboretum for stars.
- **Best Homes and Gardens:** the year-end rating is strict; your first five-star year opens the showcase house.
- **Phone calls:** a wall phone by the door of house 1 rings about once a period. Walk over in time and a caller asks for something (a discount, a rush order, a frost decision, a job seeker, a buyout offer and about 30 more, gated by level). Miss it and nothing happens.
- **Startup tax break:** your first 3 years, upkeep is half price. The welcome screen, the Ledger and the year-end screen all show how many years are left; year 3 is flagged as the last, and from year 4 you pay full upkeep.
- **Costs that grow:** crew wages rise 3% every new year, each extra greenhouse costs more upkeep than the last, and from year 4 you pay graduated income tax on operating profit (0% on the first $10,000, then 15%, 25% and 35%). The Ledger shows the running tax estimate.
- **Crowded markets:** each crop has room for about 45 flats a period. Flats past that sell for less (down to 55% of the price), and the order readout shows the drop, so a big nursery has to spread its crops across the year.
- **Late goals:** from level 10 the yearly goals are much bigger, and several must be hit two or three years running (the goal card counts the years). Rival nurseries keep improving after year 14, and the top rivals bunch up just below the ceiling instead of all sitting on it, so 2nd and 3rd place are winnable. Ranking goals count only in Prize Growers, the top magazine.
- **Greenhouses and specialty houses:** you can build up to 8 greenhouses (ground and second floor). From level 8 a house can become a **vegetable house**: vegetables grow only there, upkeep is half, vegetable jobs go 40% faster, and one olericulturist can run it alone. It takes a spring vegetable contract (the big money), a smaller fall run and herb and greens orders from grocers all year; the germination room and organic certification belong to it. From level 13 a house can become a **propagation house**: no orders, but its 8 benches stick plugs for up to 360 flats a period, ready the next period, so planting is free while its strike rate stays at 85% or more. Missed misting and cleaning lower the strike rate; below 85% it goes on probation and you pay 35% of planting costs until it runs well two periods in a row. Bought varieties owe a 10% royalty per plug until you've paid their genetics price once; showcase flowers need a propagation licence; spare plugs sell to other growers. A propagator runs it. A house has to be empty to convert, and can go back to flowers for $3,000.
- **Customers and crew:** customers' standing slips a little every period (more on higher magazine ladders, but never below Steady); full, on-time trucks keep it up. Standing sets price: Lost ×0.8, Wary ×0.88, Steady ×1, Loyal ×1.12, Partner ×1.25. An empty truck costs more reputation as the nursery grows. The crew cap allows about two crew per greenhouse, so the grower always has gaps to fill. Planning offers outnumber the benches, so choosing what to turn down matters; peak season pays ×1.3 and off-season ×0.75.
- **Each year: level up and stay healthy.** One card (in the Ledger and on the year-end screen) and one top-bar chip show both. *Level up*: the year's goal, now about growth and prestige (sales, greenhouses, specialty houses, varieties shipped, Loyal customers, reputation, magazine stars, ranking). *Stay healthy*: the year review, judged on that year alone: operating profit as a share of sales, share of trucks shipped full, the planning score and the reputation trend, with targets per level; hit 3 of 4 or the year starts over. A level goal hit on a failed try carries over: you move up straight away and replay the year at the new level, judged against the old level's review targets.
- **Pop-up orders:** a customer calls mid-period. The tablet turns orange with an "Incoming call" header and a banner, and shows only that order and the board (no other tabs), so it reads as an interruption rather than a new planning step; the clock stays paused until you place or bin it.
- **Wallet:** cash and stars sit side by side as glossy pills in the top bar (a gold coin on green, a gold star on purple) and on the tablet. They count up when they change, bump and float a "+" when you earn, and the star twinkles now and then. Star prices on homestead plots use the same gold-star-on-purple look, dimmed until you can afford them. On phones the top bar puts the title and controls on the first row and the wallet and chips on the second.
- **Year check-in:** tapping the Level up · Health chip opens just this year's card (health targets and the level-up goal), not the whole tablet. On phones the card is compact so it fits without scrolling; the Ledger's long explanation folds into "How years work".
- **Transitions:** each period ends with a fade to black and a "spring is done" card, then the planning tablet slides up; the year-end magazine slides in line by line. Pop-up buttons (OK, Continue, Close) stick to the bottom of every pop-up while you scroll.
- **No losing, just restarts:** there is no bank and no game over. If you end a year below zero, or every customer writes you off, that year starts over from the homestead. Your grower keeps the experience and skills from the failed try (and anything earned in real time). There is no bailout: you can sell your newest greenhouse for 75% of what it cost (its crew and specialty go with it; decorations go back to storage), from the restart screen or the Houses tab. Short paydays pay whoever you can, longest-unpaid first. Missed paydays can still make crew quit. Wages you haven't paid count as debt at year end, including what crew who quit are still owed (paid at the next payday you can cover). Your grower's experience slows after two levels at each nursery level, halving each level, so the grower can't race ahead of the nursery. Closing the game mid-period brings you back to that period's planning, with your orders as you left them.
- **Homestead:** between years you go home to the farm across the road. Your grower walks the paths between plots and does the clearing and building you order, one job at a time. Spend stars on clearing debris (sometimes there's a star underneath), building a display garden, farm stand, tool shed, pergola, pond, fruit trees or flower beds, growing the cat house and office, and adopting cats. Stars come from each year's Best Homes and Gardens rating, 3 more for hitting your goal, and the odd star that pops up in a greenhouse (tap it before it fades). Showcase ribbons hang on the office wall. A mailbox by the road raises its flag every 12 hours; tap it for a letter with 1 to 5 stars. Start the next year when you're ready.
- **Gift cards:** at the mailbox (or Gift cards in the Ledger tab), make a gift card once every 12 hours: a postcard of your farm and homestead with a QR code. A friend scans it in the game (live camera or a photo) or with their phone camera; they get 2 stars and you get 1. Each phone opens up to 3 gift cards every 12 hours, and each card only once. QR tools: jsQR (Apache 2.0) and QR Code Generator by Kazuhiko Arase (MIT); licences in `licenses/`.
- **Fruit trees:** shake each orchard every 6 hours for a star or two. Each orchard costs more than the last.
- **Cats:** Miley starts in the cat house. Adopt Anna, Max, Gremlin, Beatrice and Minaou as space allows; each gives a small bonus (fewer pests, fewer breakdowns, more reputation, faster new hires, better prices, more stars). They wander the greenhouses, nap, chase the odd mouse and visit their bowls. Tap one to pet it.
- **Tips:** the first time something matters, a bubble points at it and explains it, once. Key first steps are guided (year one walks you through the four health targets; year two introduces the level-up goal): the game dims everything else and waits for you to tap the highlighted spot. Readiness tips point you to features when you're ready for them (crew, upgrades, a second house, new varieties, the showcase house, a prize grower, cats). A detail panel you open at the homestead (a lot, the cat house, the mailbox) waits until the tip on screen is dealt with, then opens. Turn tips off, replay them, or start over completely from the Ledger tab.
- **People:** when you name the nursery you choose a man or a woman as your grower (you can switch when renaming). Each crew role has its own outfit: planters in aprons, waterers in bucket hats, applicators with sprayer packs and masks, mechanics in coveralls, loaders in hard hats and hi-vis vests, and the prize grower in a sun hat and gold vest. Crew members get their own skin tone and hair colour.
- **Extras:** the seasons change the ground and weather.

## Art

All graphics are vector sprites in `art.js`, generated from Python:

- `art/vector_gen.py` writes `greenhouse_vector.svg` and `greenhouse_vector.json`, a 64×64 isometric tile set.
- `art/bundle.py` packs them into `art.js`.

## Tech

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with a canvas renderer. There's no build step and no dependencies apart from Google Fonts.
